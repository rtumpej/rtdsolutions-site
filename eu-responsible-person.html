What the law says

The General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988 (GPSR) applies from 13 December 2024. A product offered online counts as made available on the EU market when the offer is targeted at consumers in the EU (Article 4).

A product covered by the GPSR may not be placed on the EU market unless an economic operator established in the EU is responsible for it (Article 16(1)). The law lists who that can be: a manufacturer established in the EU, an importer, an authorised representative with a written mandate from the manufacturer, or an EU fulfilment service provider for the products it handles (Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, Article 4(2)). MakerRep EU would be your authorised representative.

The responsible person's name and postal and electronic address must appear on the product or its packaging, the parcel or an accompanying document (Article 16(3)), and in your online offer (Article 19(b)). Online marketplaces must let you show it on each listing (Article 22(9)).

What an EU Responsible Person does

  1. Checks that the technical documentation for your product exists and makes sure it can be given to the market surveillance authorities on request.
  2. On a reasoned request from a market surveillance authority, gives it all the information and documentation needed to show that the product conforms, in a language that authority can easily understand.
  3. Informs the market surveillance authorities when it has reason to believe that a product presents a risk.
  4. Cooperates with the market surveillance authorities, including making sure that immediate corrective action is taken when it is needed.
  5. Where the risks make it appropriate, regularly checks that the product matches its technical documentation and carries the required identification, contact details, instructions and safety information, and gives the authorities documented evidence of those checks on request.
  6. Makes sure that an accident caused by the product is notified to the authorities when it learns of one.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, Article 4(3), which the GPSR applies in Article 16(1); Regulation (EU) 2023/988, Articles 16(2) and 20(4).

What stays with you as the maker

An EU Responsible Person does not take over the manufacturer's duties. Under the GPSR you still need:

  • a safe product (Article 9(1));
  • an internal risk analysis and technical documentation, with the documentation kept up to date and available for 10 years (Article 9(2) and (3));
  • a type, batch or serial number on the product, and your own name and postal and electronic address (Article 9(5) and (6));
  • clear instructions and safety information in the consumer's language, unless the product can be used safely without them (Article 9(7)).

Before we accept a product, we would go through these documents with you.

What we will take

We start with general consumer goods only: for example home décor, textiles, stationery and crafts. We will not take:

  • toys, electrical and electronic products, personal protective equipment and other products under the EU laws listed in Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, Article 4(5), which have their own conformity rules;
  • cosmetics, which need a responsible person under Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, Article 4;
  • medical devices, which need an authorised representative under Regulation (EU) 2017/745, Article 11;
  • items meant to come into contact with food, such as tableware, which have their own rules (Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004);
  • products for children, products with batteries, candles and home fragrance, and chemicals such as resins and paints;
  • food, medicines, plants and animals, which the GPSR does not cover (Article 2(2)).

From 9 December 2026, the new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853, Article 8(1)(c), makes the authorised representative of a non-EU manufacturer liable for defective products together with the manufacturer. So we will ask every maker for an indemnity and for their own product liability insurance.
